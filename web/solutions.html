Who it is for

One app that changes with the trade you are in

You tell it what kind of business you run, for the whole business or for each outlet. Screens you would never use stop taking up room, and the ones you live in come forward.

Bespoke tailors

The measurement book, the fitting diary and the balance list, in one place that cannot go home in somebody's bag.

  • Measurements per customer, with body notes and every piece you have made for them.
  • Fittings on the calendar, with the customer messaged when the stage moves.
  • Deposits and balances to the naira, and what is overdue surfaced without you looking.
  • Job sheets for the bench with the measurements printed on the page.
See it on the demo

Shoe makers

Made to order work that does not go through cutting and sewing. Name your own stages and the board follows them.

  • Your stages, your words. Clicking, lasting, soling, finishing, whatever the bench actually calls them.
  • Leather and components as stock, counted in hides, pairs, sheets or pieces.
  • Size and fitting notes on the order, next to the photographs of the pair.
  • Cost per pair, so you know what a style really makes before you price the next one.

Straight answer: the measurement fields are written for garments today, so foot measurements live in the order notes. If you make shoes and want proper fields, say so and it moves up the list.

Bag and leather makers

Small runs, one offs and repeat styles, with the materials each one ate accounted for properly.

  • Styles you repeat, with their components and their true cost saved.
  • Hides, hardware and linings counted separately, with reorder levels.
  • Made to order and off the shelf in the same list, because most benches do both.
  • Batches, so a run of twenty moves through the board as one job.
See it on the demo

Ready to wear and boutiques

Sell off the rail without a measurement screen in sight. Tell it you are retail and the workroom tools step out of the way.

  • Counter sales in a few taps, with the receipt on your own letterhead.
  • Stock by size and colour, coming down as it goes out of the door.
  • What is actually selling, by piece, by category and by outlet.
  • Customers who come back, with what they bought and when they last did.
See it on the demo

Fabric shops

Stock that is sold by length, counted the way you actually count it, with the offcuts accounted for.

  • By the yard or the metre, in bolts or cut lengths, whichever the shop works in.
  • Price per length, with a customer's cut recorded against the roll it came off.
  • What is running low before a regular walks in for the last six yards.
  • Suppliers, what you paid, and what you still owe them.
See it on the demo

Haberdashery

Hundreds of small lines that move fast and disappear quietly. This is the part a notebook never survives.

  • Threads, zips, buttons, trims, counted in spools, packs, cards or pieces.
  • Reorder levels per line, so the fast movers are on the shelf when they are asked for.
  • Margin per line, which on small items is where the money quietly leaks.
  • Sell to the trade as well as over the counter, with what each workroom owes you.
See it on the demo

Made to measure and both at once

A workroom at the back and a rail at the front. Most businesses that last end up here, and the two sides are counted separately and reported together.

  • Both kinds of sale in one list, each with its own profit line.
  • One stock room feeding the bench and the shop floor, counted once.
  • Every outlet its own type, so a Lagos workroom and an Abuja shop can sit under one account.
  • One set of books across all of it, split by outlet whenever you want.
See it on the demo
Whatever you are

The parts nobody escapes

The trade decides what the front of the app looks like. What sits underneath is the same, because every business here has to answer the same three questions at the end of the month.

Who owes me

Balances, overdue, and which customer to ring first, without opening a spreadsheet.

What did I make

Real books, profit per sale, and what the month actually retained after everything.

Who did the work

Wages that follow the work, whether that is a salary, a piece rate or a commission.

Not sure which one you are?

Most businesses here are two of these at once. Tell us what you actually do in a sentence and we will tell you honestly whether this fits.
