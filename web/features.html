The product

Everything your business runs on, in one app

Twenty two screens that already know about each other. Pick the part of the business you want to see.

Back to the overview

An order that carries everything about itself

Made to order or sold over the counter, both live here. A made to order job holds its pieces, its costs, the deposit, the balance, who is making it and the measurements it was cut to. A sale holds what left the shelf and who bought it.

  • Deposits and balances to the naira, with what is overdue surfaced without you looking.
  • Invoices, receipts and job sheets printed on your own letterhead.
  • Measurements kept per customer, with body notes and every piece you have ever made for them.
  • Counter sales and delivery as well as made to order, with the courier and who signed for it.

The month, and what it is holding you to

Fittings, appointments, the day an order is due and the day a delivery goes out, on one month at a time rather than in four different places.

  • Fittings booked against the customer, so the measurements and the pieces are already there when they walk in.
  • Due dates from the orders themselves, not typed in twice and left to drift apart.
  • Deliveries on the same month, so the workroom and the road are not planned separately.
  • Only what you are allowed to see. A maker's calendar shows their own work, not the whole label's.

A board that answers the only question that matters today

What is late, what is close, and who is holding it. The stages are yours to name, whether the work goes through cutting and sewing or clicking, lasting and soling, and the customer record, the calendar and the dashboard all move with it.

  • Assigned by person, so a maker opens the app and sees their own pile.
  • Late work is loud, colour coded rather than buried in a report.
  • A status change is the moment to message the customer, and the message is ready to send.
  • Follow up built from people who have already paid you, not a bought list.

The shelf and the screen agree

Cloth by the yard, shoes by the pair, buttons by the card, ready to wear by the size. Count it in whatever unit you actually buy and sell in. When a job uses it or a customer takes it, it comes off the count.

  • Low stock warnings before you find out at the cutting table.
  • Cost carried through, so what a job or a sale consumed shows up in its profit.
  • Vendors and suppliers with what you buy, what you paid and what you still owe.

It is not finished until it is in their hands

Dispatch, the courier who took it, and who signed for it at the other end. A piece that has left the workroom is still your problem until that last part is filled in.

  • Every dispatch on a record, with the courier, the date and the person who received it.
  • Courier performance, so you can see which one actually delivers on time before you book them again.
  • Counter sales and deliveries together, because a sale off the rail and a piece sent across Lagos are both money owed until they land.
  • Per outlet, so a Lagos dispatch and an Abuja one are not counted against each other.

Real books, not a list of payments

Every order, wage, roll of fabric and expense lands in the same ledger, so the profit figure is arrived at rather than guessed.

Profit per order

Value less fabric, less labour, less commission and your own allocation. You see which jobs are worth taking again.

Money in by channel

Cash received tagged by where it came from and how it was paid, so the studio, Instagram and WhatsApp are separate lines.

Expenses and funds

Recurring costs, one offs, receipts attached, and money set aside for fabric or rent so you know what is genuinely spare.

How the owner gets paid

Your draw set once, as a share of profit or value, a fixed amount per order, or a monthly salary. Every new order follows it.

Reports you can hand over

Period by period, branch by branch, with retained profit carried properly. What an accountant or a lender asks for.

Invoices and receipts

On your letterhead, with your logo and account details, ready the moment the order is taken.

Pay that follows the work, worked out for you

Salary, piece rate, commission or a mix. Set the rate once per person and the app adds up what they are owed from the jobs they actually finished, with payslips at the end of it.

  • Rota and attendance, so hours worked are recorded rather than remembered.
  • Leave requested, approved and counted against a balance.
  • Branches with their own staff, stock and takings, rolled up when you want the whole picture.
  • Hidden until you need it. A one person shop never sees any of this. It appears when you hire.

The next order is usually from someone you have already served

Your customer list is the only list worth having, and the app already knows which part of it to talk to this week. It picks the people and writes the message. You send it yourself.

  • Groups it works out for you: your top tier, everyone who owes a balance, the new ones this month, and the ones who have not been back in ninety days.
  • The message written for you for a newsletter, an announcement, a promotion or a launch, ready to paste into your email or your WhatsApp broadcast.
  • Only people who have bought from you, with an email or a WhatsApp number they gave you. Nothing here is a bought list.
  • Sending is still yours. Automatic sending is on the way and is marked as such below, so nothing here pretends to be finished.

Who did what, and who is allowed to

The part you only think about after something has gone missing. Six roles, a record of every change, and a board for the things everybody needs to know.

  • Six roles with real permissions. A cutter cannot open payroll, and a branch manager sees their own outlet rather than the whole label.
  • An audit trail you can search, so who changed the price and who marked it paid both have a name and a time against them.
  • Announcements and tasks, so a thing that has to happen sits against a person rather than in a WhatsApp group nobody scrolls back through.
  • Your data stays yours. Everything is exportable, any day you ask, in a file you can open anywhere.

It does not stop when the network does

Install it from the browser and it sits on your home screen like any other app. With no signal it still opens, still saves and still prints. When you are back it catches up on its own.

  • Android, iPhone, Windows and Mac, with nothing to download from a store.
  • Saved on the device first, so a dropped line never costs you an order.
  • Installs to the home screen and opens like any other app, with no store, no update to chase and no download over data you are paying for.
  • Built for a phone first. A table drops columns as the screen narrows rather than turning into a wall of cards you cannot scan.

Built for a real Lagos day

Power goes. Data finishes. A customer is standing in front of you and the page will not load. Everything here assumes that happens and keeps going anyway, because software that only works on a good day is not software a business can lean on.

Not only for tailors

Tell it what kind of business you are, and it changes

You pick this when you set up, and again for each outlet if you have more than one. What you never use stops taking up room.

Made to order

Bespoke clothing, shoes, bags. Measurements, a board with your own stages, fittings and balances.

Ready to wear

Sell off the rail. Counter sales, stock by size, and no measurement screens in the way.

Both at once

A workroom at the back and a rail at the front, counted separately and reported together.

Fabrics

Sold by length. Count and price by the yard or the metre, in bolts or cut pieces.

Haberdashery

Trims, notions, threads, zips and buttons, in the units you actually buy them in.

One of each

Every branch has its own type, so a bespoke studio, an outlet and a fabric shop can sit under one account.
